title: Does quantity improve quality?

---

> […] quantity and quality are not opposing forces.
> You don't have to sacrifice one to get more of the other.

When I've listened to those words in a [podcast](https://open.spotify.com/episode/3toZQK2jtxR2U5UIEarjpp?si=8mLvPyNTQIWcVi9VwprbKQ), I wasn't sure how I felt about it.

A part of me was very aligned with the concept. Writing regularly have improved my writing for example. Quantity improving quality. But on the other hand, as someone working in product, my job involves rejecting hundreds of good ideas to protect teams' focus. Protecting quality by limiting quantity.

After some considerations, I've come to the conclusion that the key difference is in the mode we are in: project-mode vs art-mode

### Project-mode

In project-mode, you have an objective and several constraints. The project management triangle, which has its own dedicated [Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management_triangle), reminds us that "*Quality is constrained by cost, scope and time*". 

We use it constantly in product discussion: so, you want to add this fancy new feature as a requirement? Sure. But the size of our team is fixed. And, we won't lower the quality of what we're building. So, to add this feature, we'll have to delay the release. Are you sure this is what you want? Really, really want?

Of course, reality is more nuanced than that. For example, raising the cost by hiring new team members won't make your feature ready faster. You can make a baby in one month with 9 women, mythical-man month, you know that.

But still, in our daily-life, we spend a lot of time in project-mode, and here, **quantity reduces quality**. When we're doing dishes, or peeling vegetables: going faster won't make it better.

### Art-mode

But there is more in life than deadlines and objectives. Art for example. Creativity.
When you're creating, something counter-intuitive happens: **quantity increases quality**.

As Rick Rubin puts it in The Creative Act, making art is connecting to the endless creativity of the universe. In that sense, creativity is like a river: once it flows, it keeps flowing. But if it stalls, sediments start to accumulate, and flow diminishes.

This is why when we're creating, the most important thing is to keep our creativity flowing. The moment we feel a slowdown, we should redirect our creativity somewhere else, anywhere it doesn't matter. It just have to keep flowing. Like rivers creating new meanders.

This [story](https://seths.blog/2009/12/fear-of-bad-ideas/) by Seth Godin, is a typical illustration what art-mode look likes :
> Someone asked me where I get all my good ideas, explaining that it takes him a month or two to come up with one and I seem to have more than that. I asked him how many bad ideas he has every month. He paused and said, "none."

Art-mode, our goal is to produce as much art as possible, keep the creativity flowing. For the hope that sometimes we will create something brilliant. Or maybe because it is the only way to do that.
